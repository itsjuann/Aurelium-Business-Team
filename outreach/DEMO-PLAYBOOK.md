## The one rule

**They call the receptionist before Juan explains anything.** If they will not dial, Juan dials on speaker. A demo they hear beats a demo they are told about. Demo agents use fictional business data only; never demo on a real client's agent.

## The 15-minute structure

| Minute | What happens | Juan says |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Thank, confirm the 15 minutes, one line on who we are. Give the demo number. | "Thanks for making time. I'm Juan, I run Aurelium Motion here in Philly. Before I explain anything, call this number and ask it whatever a new patient would ask you. I'll stay on mute." |
| 2–5 | They call. Juan listens, says nothing until they hang up. | "What did you notice?" (then silence; let them talk) |
| 5–10 | Discovery. Their numbers, out loud. | The five questions below, in order, skipping any they already answered. Write the numbers down and repeat them back. |
| 10–13 | How it would work for them. | Their hours, their top three call types, booking into their system or message-taking, the escalation rule they just described, live in 5 business days, Juan reviews the first 20 calls personally. State the honest-AI rule before they ask: "If a caller asks, it says it's the practice's AI receptionist. It never pretends to be a person." |
| 13–15 | Next step. | "If this covers the gap while you keep hiring, I'll send a one-page proposal tonight. Which fits better: capture and message, or full booking into your calendar?" Then pricing, plainly, from the business file (once confirmed), next to the cost of the role they are hiring for. Agree a date for the yes/no. |

Timing guard: if discovery runs long, cut minutes 10–13 to two sentences and protect the next step. Never skip the live call.

## Discovery questions (the five that do the work)

1. "Walk me through what happens to a call at 12:30 on a Tuesday." (surfaces lunch coverage, voicemail, who is double-tasking)
2. "How many voicemails did you get last week, and how many got a call back the same day?" (the leak, in their numbers)
3. "What is a new patient worth to the practice in year one?" (lets them do the math; for ortho, ask about a case start instead)
4. "How long has the front-desk role been open, and who is covering the phone meanwhile?" (the hiring-gap pain and the cost of the last hire)
5. "When the AI doesn't know the answer, what would you want it to do?" (writes the escalation rule and dissolves the fear)

Follow-ups if there is time: "Which calls do you never want it to handle?" · "What do new patients ask most: insurance, hours, parking, cost?" · "What scheduling system are you on?" · "Do you want after-hours only, overflow only, or every call first?"

Quantify out loud, with their numbers only: voicemails per week × share that are new patients × value per new patient. Never supply the numbers for them and never cite another practice's results (we have none measured yet).

## Objection bank (dental and orthodontic)

| "What if it says something wrong?" | "It only answers from the FAQ you approve: hours, location, services, insurance you take, parking. Our data-protection policy for dental practices is shortest retention, minimal fields, audio off unless you sign for it, and the receptionist is scripted not to collect symptoms or treatment details. Whether we sign a BAA is something I'll answer in writing with the proposal." Then hand to `client-data-protection`. | Any BAA promise until the [CONFIRM] in the business file is resolved. |
| "I'd rather hire a person." | "Keep hiring. This covers the phone until you do, and after that it covers lunch, after-hours and the days your person is out sick. It is a receptionist, not a replacement for your team." | "You don't need a front desk." |

## Demo-call scenarios (fictional data only)

The demo agent represents a fictional practice. Suggested scenarios for the prospect to try, matched to their vertical:

- **General dentistry**: "Do you take Keystone insurance?" → "I chipped a tooth, can you see me today?" → "Is there parking?"
- **Orthodontics**: "How much are braces for a 12-year-old?" (should give a range or offer a consult, never a quote the practice did not approve) → "Do you do Invisalign?" → "My bracket came off, what do I do?"
- **Pediatric dental**: "What age do you start seeing kids?" → "Can I book two siblings back to back?"
- **Edge cases worth showing**: caller asks "Am I talking to a real person?" (hear the honest answer); caller describes an emergency (hear the escalation); caller asks something outside the FAQ (hear the message-taking).

Receptionist Builder confirms before each demo that the dental demo scenario exists on an agent with fictional data and a dialable number.
