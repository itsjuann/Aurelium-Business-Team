## Two calls, not one
A $15,000 decision is not made on a cold call. The cold call books Call 1.

**Call 1: discovery (30 minutes).** Their numbers only. No demo, no price.
1. "About how many calls come in on a normal week? On the first cold week?"
2. "How many of those hit voicemail: after hours, weekends, when every line is busy?"
3. "What is an average service call worth? An install?"
4. "When someone gets an estimate and does not book, what happens next?"
5. "How many past customers have not heard from you since their last job?"
6. "Who answers the phone today, and what does that cost you a month?"
7. "What do you run dispatch on: ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, something else? Which plan?" (Decides how the integration is built. Ask before promising a go-live date.)
8. "Which towns do you actually run trucks in?" (This becomes their exclusive area.)

Do the math out loud with their answers. Then: "Let me build this with {{company}}'s name, your services and your service-call fee. Thursday you call it yourself. If it does not sound like your shop, we stop."

**Between the calls (Juan + Receptionist Builder, 1–2 days).** Build their demo: the receptionist answering as their company, and the demo CRM showing the booked job. Use fictional customers only.

**Call 2: the $15k call (45 minutes).**
1. **Recap their numbers (5 min).** Read back what they said, in their words.
2. **They call it (10 min).** They say the heat is out. The job lands on the calendar and in the CRM while they watch. Then show the estimate follow-up, the tune-up campaign and the review request firing.
3. **The whole system (10 min).** Walk the stack above, each piece tied to a leak they named on Call 1. Skip pieces they did not care about. Always say these three out loud: it books into the software they already use, they are the only HVAC shop in their towns that gets it, and the guarantee is in writing.
4. **The math (5 min).** Missed calls per month × their booking rate × their average ticket, plus one install. Their numbers, not ours.
5. **The offer (5 min).** "It is $15,000 to build it, plus a one-time $2,500 setup fee. After that it is $1,200 a month to run it, tune it and keep the campaigns going. In year one that is about $2,660 a month all in, less than a CSR, and it works nights and weekends. From year two it is $1,200 a month."
6. **The close (10 min).** "We can be live in 14 days. Do you want to start Monday?" Then stop talking.

## Honest limits
- No HVAC client is live yet, so there is no case study. Never imply one. The demo and the written guarantee carry the proof.
- The full system is a 14-day build with a website and search work in it. Two builds at once is a sensible ceiling for one person until the HVAC template exists.
- **Integration is the piece most likely to slip.** How it connects depends on the shop's software and plan. Find that out on Call 1, and do not promise the 14 days until you know the route works. If a direct connection is not possible, say so and agree the fallback (for example, the job arrives by email or text to the dispatcher) before they sign.
- **The guarantee is a real liability.** It is only safe if every booked job is recorded with its source from day one. The dashboard and monthly report are how it gets proved.
- **Exclusivity shrinks the market.** Each client takes their towns off the table, so sign the larger shops in each area first.
