## Two calls, not one
A $15,000 decision is not made on a cold call. The cold call books Call 1.

**Call 1: discovery (30 minutes).** Their numbers only. No demo, no price.
1. "About how many calls come in on a normal week? On the first cold week?"
2. "How many of those hit voicemail: after hours, weekends, when every line is busy?"
3. "What is an average service call worth? An install?"
4. "When someone gets an estimate and does not book, what happens next?"
5. "How many past customers have not heard from you since their last job?"
6. "Who answers the phone today, and what does that cost you a month?"

Do the math out loud with their answers. Then: "Let me build this with {{company}}'s name, your services and your service-call fee. Thursday you call it yourself. If it does not sound like your shop, we stop."

**Between the calls (Juan + Receptionist Builder, 1–2 days).** Build their demo: the receptionist answering as their company, and the demo CRM showing the booked job. Use fictional customers only.

**Call 2: the $15k call (45 minutes).**
1. **Recap their numbers (5 min).** Read back what they said, in their words.
2. **They call it (10 min).** They say the heat is out. The job lands on the calendar and in the CRM while they watch. Then show the estimate follow-up, the tune-up campaign and the review request firing.
3. **The whole system (10 min).** Walk the stack above, each piece tied to a leak they named on Call 1. Skip pieces they did not care about.
4. **The math (5 min).** Missed calls per month × their booking rate × their average ticket, plus one install. Their numbers, not ours.
5. **The offer (5 min).** "It is $25,000 for the year, everything in it. $15,000 gets the build started, and the rest is spread across the year at about $833 a month. There is a one-time $2,500 setup fee. All in, that is about $2,290 a month, less than a CSR, and it works nights and weekends."
6. **The close (10 min).** "We can be live in 14 days. Do you want to start Monday?" Then stop talking.
